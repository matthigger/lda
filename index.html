LDA, visually

Find the line that pulls two classes apart, then the Bayes boundary it comes from: straight when the classes share a covariance (LDA), curved when they don't (QDA).

Which line separates best?

Goal: describe each 2-d sample by one number, its score t = wT(x − m) along a unit direction w, and classify by a threshold τ on t, with the two classes' scores overlapping as little as possible.

Approach: (Fisher 1936) push the projected class means apart while keeping each projected class narrow: a large projected mean difference relative to the within-class scatter along w.

maxw J(w) = (wTΔ)2wTΣw ⇒ w ∝ Σ−1Δ,   Δ = μ1 − μ0

Δ is the mean difference and Σ the covariance within each class, shared by both (with 1/n); each class's 40 samples have exactly mean μk and covariance Σ, and m = (μ0 + μ1)/2. J ignores the length of w; its best value J* = ΔTΣ−1Δ = D2 is the squared Mahalanobis distance between the means.

Bayes boundary: LDA → QDA

Goal: predict the class with the larger posterior P(y = k | x) ∝ P(y = k) N(x; μk, Σk), the Bayes classifier: when the Gaussians are right, no rule has a lower error.

Approach: the sign of the log-odds, with Dk2 = (x − μk)TΣk−1(x − μk) the squared Mahalanobis distance to each mean. With one shared Σ the quadratic terms cancel and the boundary g = 0 is a line (LDA); otherwise it is a conic (QDA).

g(x) = ½D02 − ½D12 − ½ ln|Σ1||Σ0| + lnP(y = 1)P(y = 0) ⇒Σ0 = Σ1: g(x) = wTx + b, w = Σ−1(μ1 − μ0)

You set the true Gaussians and prior; n training samples are drawn from them. LDA and QDA fit the means, priors and covariances (maximum likelihood, 1/n) to the training samples only, then classify by the sign of their own g.

Try this

  • Pick a bit of both and press w = Δ, then w = Σ's short axis, then w = Σ−1Δ. Compare J.
    AnswerΔ has the largest projected mean difference and Σ's short axis the smallest within-class scatter, yet both score J = 7.2. Σ−1Δ = [2, −2] gives up a little of each and scores J* = 12.
  • Pick skinny and press w = Δ. Why not just use the mean difference?
    AnswerThe means differ equally in both features, but the horizontal variance is 10 and the vertical only 0.1. Along Δ the horizontal noise swamps the mean difference: J is about 4% of the best. Σ−1 discounts the noisy feature, so the best w is nearly vertical.
  • On tilted, press w = v₁ (PCA). How well does PCA's top direction separate the classes?
    AnswerBadly: about 9% of the best J, with the bumps piled on top of each other. PCA never looks at the labels, so it picks the direction of most total variance, here the long axis both clouds share. The classes differ across that axis.
  • With share Σ on, slide P(y = 1). What does the boundary do?
    AnswerIt slides but never turns, toward the rarer class: the prior is only in b, through ln(P(y = 1) / P(y = 0)). Its normal w = Σ−1Δ is the Fisher direction from the first tab.
  • Switch the plot to 3D, set the height to ln P(y = k) p(x | y = k), and compare shared Σ with one wider. Where does the boundary come from, and why is it straight in one and curved in the other?
    AnswerBayes picks the class whose surface is on top, so the boundary is where the surfaces cross. Each is −½Dk2 + const, an upside-down bowl shaped by Σk. With one shared Σ the bowls have the same shape, so their difference g has no quadratic terms: a tilted plane, which crosses zero in a line. With different Σk the difference stays quadratic and the crossing is a conic, here an ellipse around the tight class.
  • Stay on one wider and pick surfaces from: LDA fit. Why does LDA draw a line here?
    AnswerLDA gives both classes one pooled Σ̂, so its two bowls always have the same shape and always cross in a line, even when the true classes (the black curve on the floor) call for a curve.
  • On shared Σ, set n = 10 and press New samples a few times. Compare LDA and QDA on train and on test, then raise n to 400.
    AnswerAt n = 10, QDA does at least as well as LDA on the training samples almost every time, yet worse on test almost every time: it fits 11 numbers (two means, two covariances, a prior) to LDA's 8, and the extra 3 chase noise. The truth here has one Σ, so LDA is the right model. With 400 samples both land near the Bayes error.
