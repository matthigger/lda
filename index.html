LDA, visually

Find the line that pulls two classes apart, then the Bayes boundary it comes from: straight when the classes share a covariance (LDA), curved when they don't (QDA).

Which line separates best?

Goal: describe each 2-d sample by one number, its score t = wT(x − m) along a unit direction w, and classify by a threshold τ on t, with the two classes' scores overlapping as little as possible.

Approach: (Fisher) push the projected class means apart while keeping each projected class narrow: a big gap relative to the spread.

maxw J(w) = (wT(μ1 − μ0))2wTΣw ⇒ w ∝ Σ−1(μ1 − μ0)

Σ is the covariance within each class, shared by both (with 1/n); each class's 40 samples have exactly mean μk and covariance Σ, and m = (μ0 + μ1)/2. J ignores the length of w; its best value J* = (μ1 − μ0)TΣ−1(μ1 − μ0) is the squared Mahalanobis distance between the means.

Bayes boundary: LDA → QDA

Goal: predict the class with the larger posterior P(y = k | x) ∝ πk N(x; μk, Σk), the Bayes classifier: when the Gaussians are right, no rule has a lower error.

Approach: the sign of the log-odds, with dk2 = (x − μk)TΣk−1(x − μk) the squared Mahalanobis distance to each mean. With one shared Σ the quadratic terms cancel and the boundary g = 0 is a line (LDA); otherwise it is a conic (QDA).

g(x) = ½d02 − ½d12 − ½ log|Σ1||Σ0| + logπ1π0 ⇒Σ0 = Σ1: g(x) = wTx + b, w = Σ−1(μ1 − μ0)

You set the true Gaussians and prior; n training samples are drawn from them. LDA and QDA fit the means, priors and covariances (maximum likelihood, 1/n) to the training samples only, then classify by the sign of their own g.

Try this

  • Press Sweep w and watch the two histograms. Where is J highest?
    AnswerAt w ∝ Σ−1(μ1 − μ0) (or its negative), the peak of the curve: there the two bumps are as far apart as they can be, measured in widths. Setting ∇J = 0 gives Σw ∝ μ1 − μ0.
  • Pick skinny (the notebook's last example) and press w = μ₁ − μ₀. Why not just use the difference of the means?
    AnswerThe means differ equally in both features, but the horizontal variance is 10 and the vertical only 0.1. Along μ1 − μ0 the horizontal noise swamps the gap: J is about 4% of the best. Σ−1 discounts the noisy feature, so the best w is nearly vertical.
  • Try ICA part 1 and part 2. Why is μ1 − μ0 already the best direction there?
    AnswerBecause μ1 − μ0 is an eigenvector of Σ (in part 2 every direction is, since Σ = I), so Σ−1 only rescales it.
  • On tilted, press w = v₁ (PCA). How well does PCA's top direction separate the classes?
    AnswerBadly: about 9% of the best J, with the bumps piled on top of each other. PCA never looks at the labels, so it picks the direction of most total spread, here the long axis both clouds share. The classes differ across that axis.
  • At the best w, drag τ. Where are the fewest training errors?
    AnswerNear τ = 0, the midpoint m: the classes are the same size and the same width along w, so the bumps cross halfway between their means. The next tab shows that this line is the Bayes boundary for equal priors.
  • With share Σ on, slide π1. What does the boundary do?
    AnswerIt slides but never turns, toward the rarer class: the prior is only in b, through log(π1 / π0). Its normal w = Σ−1(μ1 − μ0) is the Fisher direction from the first tab.
  • Pick one wider. What shape is the Bayes boundary, and why?
    AnswerAn ellipse around the tight class 0. Far from μ0 in any direction, the wide class is the more likely one, so class 0 only wins on an island around its mean.
  • Pick crossed, then same mean. How does LDA do?
    AnswerAbout as well as a coin flip, or always guessing the bigger class. The means match, so μ̂1 − μ̂0 is just noise and LDA's line is nearly arbitrary. The classes differ only in their covariances, which only QDA uses: a hyperbola for crossed, a circle for same mean.
  • Back on shared Σ, set n = 10 and press New samples a few times. Compare LDA and QDA on train and on test, then raise n to 400.
    AnswerAt n = 10, QDA does at least as well as LDA on the training samples almost every time, yet worse on test almost every time: it fits 11 numbers (two means, two covariances, a prior) to LDA's 8, and the extra 3 chase noise. The truth here has one Σ, so LDA is the right model. With 400 samples both land near the Bayes error.
  • Hover a point on the Bayes boundary. What do d02 and d12 show?
    AnswerWith a shared Σ and equal priors they are equal: the boundary is the set of points equally far from both means, measured in Σ's units rather than in plain distance.
